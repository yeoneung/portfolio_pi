CORRELATED ASSET RETURNS ON AN IRREGULAR FACTOR NETWORK

Model
-----
There are d predictive state variables and d risky assets, d=10,20,50.
The asset covariance is I+A/10, where A is the adjacency matrix of the
undirected binary tree with zero-based parent(i)=(i-1)//2, i=1,...,d-1.
Thus adjacent asset returns have correlation 0.1. The graph is connected;
it has d-1 edges and vertices of degrees 1, 2, and 3.

The state interaction matrix is M=A/4+diag(1-degree(i)/4). It is symmetric,
entrywise nonnegative, and doubly stochastic. State drift is (-I+M/5)z,
state covariance is I/16, and asset/state cross covariance is I/8.
The joint covariance is positive definite: the lower row-sum bound for
asset covariance is at least 0.7, while chi^2/Q=0.25.

Risk aversion is gamma=3, r=3/100, T=1, and the cube radius is 3/2.
The exposure constraints are |pi_i|<=11/(100 sqrt(d)). For zero-based i,
s_i=2*i/(d-1)-1 and t_i=2*((3*i+1) mod d)/(d-1)-1. Excess returns are
  [ell_i+a_i*z_i+b_i*(M z)_i]/sqrt(d),
  ell_i=(1+s_i/5)/5, a_i=(1+t_i/5)/2, b_i=(1-s_i/5)/4.
Every intercept is distinct, and the predictor matrix is full rank by
strict diagonal dominance. Independent state noise is present in every
coordinate; this is not a one-state model with many assets.

Deployed policy and candidates
-----------------------------
Each coordinate has its own 15-coefficient polynomial
  f_i(tau,x)=tau sum_{j=0}^4 T_j(2*tau-1)*(c_0j+c_1j*x+c_2j*x^2).
The factor candidate is exp((1-gamma)*r*tau+sum_i f_i(tau,z_i)/d).
The generating coefficients f_0,i define the feasible componentwise rule
  pi_i=clip((ell_i+a_i*z_i+b_i*(M z)_i+chi*f_0,i,x/sqrt(d))/gamma,
            -11/100,11/100)/sqrt(d).
This componentwise rule is not asserted to solve the covariance-metric
greedy QP. Its welfare is certified against the optimal policy for the
full correlated covariance I+A/10. It is zero outside the cube and is
reapplied at re-entry. Separate evaluation leaves this rule unchanged.

GPU coefficient proposals use the actual correlated fixed-policy generator,
float64 arithmetic, seed 1110, and 1200 Adam updates on an RTX 5070.
Each update uses 2048 joint-state draws, mixing 97% from the stationary
physical Gaussian distribution with 3% uniform on the cube.
Sampled fitting never supplies the certificate.

The two evaluation polynomials at each coordinate are fitted by three
sequential linear programs. A representative homogeneous proposal supplies
common potentials phi_L,phi_U; these potentials are held fixed during the
coordinate-dependent evaluation fits. Each potential has five Chebyshev
time coefficients multiplying x and one constant coefficient multiplying
x^2. The quadratic coefficient is nonpositive for phi_L and nonnegative
for phi_U. Initial proposals use 13 time nodes and 37 nodes for each of
x,y; refinement uses 25 time nodes and 73 spatial nodes. Verified dyadic
time shifts can enforce the requested tolerance without changing spatial
derivatives. All final coefficients are exact multiples of 2^-36.

Proof behind verification
-------------------------
Let s_i^-=1-degree(i)/10 and s_i^+=1+degree(i)/10. The exact inequality
2|v_i*v_j|<=v_i^2+v_j^2 gives
  diag(s_i^-) <= Sigma <= diag(s_i^+)
in the positive-semidefinite order. This bounds the original coupled
quadratic control term; the stochastic market covariance is not replaced.
Define
  F_i(f,p;s)=f_tau+(x-y/5)*f_x-Q*f_xx/2-Q*f_x^2/(2d)
           +(gamma-1)*[p*(ell_i+a_i*x+b_i*y+chi*f_x/sqrt(d))
                        -gamma*s*p^2/2].
The normalized Bellman residual is at most the average of
max_{|p|<=11/100} F_i(f_L,i,p;s_i^-). The normalized fixed-policy residual
is at least the average of F_i(f_U,i,p_0,i;s_i^+).

For y_i=(M z)_i, concavity and double stochasticity give
  sum_i [phi_L(tau,y_i)-phi_L(tau,z_i)] >= 0.
Convexity gives the reverse inequality for phi_U. Thus continuous bounds
on F_i+phi(tau,y)-phi(tau,x), on the rectangle [-R,R]^2, imply global
residual bounds in d states. This also explains why three-variable local
enclosures remain sufficient on a branching graph. Every coordinate
cylinder is checked separately; no rotational-symmetry reuse occurs.

Each check retains all compatible clipping branches, enforces the exact
terminal trace, and encloses the lateral trace differences divided by tau.
The code uses 60-digit interval coefficient algebra, rational Bernstein
transforms, and outward-rounded binary64 de Casteljau subdivision.
All accepted dyadic cells together have exact volume one; the residual
and trace tolerances are 1/1000. Each exponent uses its actual verified
coordinate correction, averaged over all d coordinates.

The exit estimate is uniform over admissible controls. Its stopped
coordinate drift shift is epsilon*R+(gamma-1)*chi*cap/sqrt(d); it uses
128 time bins and the exponential martingale inequality. Continuation
uses the weight exp(|z|^2/(20d)), the full covariance upper bound, and
the generator constants in the network-comparison proposition.
The whole-space lower and upper factors include this exit correction.
For gamma=3 the CE-loss upper bound is 10000*(1-sqrt(lower/upper)) bp.

Reproduction
------------
The one-year whole-space CE upper bounds, rounded upward, are 61.425 bp
at d=10, 63.248 bp at d=20, and 64.896 bp at d=50. Accepted cell counts
are 46,127, 96,995, and 256,066. Serial verification times for the supplied
records are 7.73, 15.95, and 41.45 seconds, respectively.

From the code root, in a copy if the original manifest is to be preserved:
  python experiments/highdim_network.py --dimensions 10 20 50
  python analysis/report_highdim_network.py
  python -m pytest tests/test_highdim_network.py -q
Regenerate proposals and verify them:
  python experiments/highdim_network.py --fit --dimensions 10 20 50

Candidate files contain all market matrices and exact polynomial coefficients.
Certificate files contain every coordinate cover, localization constants,
factor endpoints, input/source hashes, and CPU verification times. Summary
and table generation recheck hashes and independently repeat the interval
endpoint assembly. Table times sum the serial verification of all final
coordinate candidates. Gaussian training and sampled LP proposals are
excluded from these verification times.

Mathematical conventions checked against standard sources: positive-
semidefinite order and Jensen's inequality, Boyd and Vandenberghe,
Convex Optimization (2004), Chapters 2 and 3:
https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/
The required matrix and network inequalities are proved explicitly above.
